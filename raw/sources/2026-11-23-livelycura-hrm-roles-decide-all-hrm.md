# LivelyCura HRM: "roles decide" widened to all of HRM (decided 2026-11-23)

- Date: 2026-11-23
- Author: the product owner, replying in a LivelyCura Claude Code session
- Recorded from: the owner's own message (quoted verbatim below) and the session's context
- Feature: LivelyCura HRM approvals and actions (open-shift pickups, overtime, leave, and the rest of HRM), with who is told about them
- Status when recorded: decided; checking the existing approvals against it is pending

## Context

The session had put a plan to the owner for the follow-up "First-come pickups have no manager safeguards": tell every pickup approver about each automatic pickup, and make pickups past 12 hours in a day, or whose overtime can't be paid, wait for an approver even in first-come mode. On 2026-11-21 the owner had chosen "roles decide" (HR-led through roles) for shift actions.

## The owner's answer (verbatim)

- "what you brought makes sense to but you have also know the approving also shifts should also work exactly like what we did earlier concerning how shifts are assigned, I am talking about HR-led through roles, cus hr is supposed to accept overtime requests and stuff like even leave request. Let's say anything in hrm if the company has hrm team the handle everything and if the company don't have hr team and they want the branch managers to handle that then they assign them permissions for those and they perform the actions for them"

## The rule as decided

- Every HRM approval and action, including approving open-shift pickups, overtime and leave, follows "roles decide": a company with an HR team gives HR organization-wide roles and HR handles everything; a company without one gives branch managers the permissions at their branch and they act instead.
- This widens the 2026-11-21 shift-management decision from shift actions to all of HRM.
- The session's reading: who is told about a request (inbox and push) follows the same rule, so everyone holding the permission at that branch, organization-wide holders included, is told.
